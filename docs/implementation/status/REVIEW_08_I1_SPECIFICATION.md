## 7. Real-time feasibility and Rust readiness

A causal implementation can prepare shared FIR coefficients, history and fixed-size per-lane arrays off thread. Projection admits bounded chronological work, with at most 84 order/index terms per host sample, publication work for up to 12 orders, and bounded support scans. For four carriers the proposal costs 96 complex section updates plus 48 weighted terms and about 99 real monitor accumulations per lane/tick, and a trial per tick over 17 ticks. At 192 kHz stereo, the sections alone consume about 36.9 million complex updates/s, before guide, projection, monitor, denormal handling and control changes. Instantaneous FIR, launch and closing/publication work can coincide on a block of one sample. An FFT, full saved-window resynthesis or lookahead on audio is unnecessary. This is bounded work with a plausible causal realization, with real-time headroom still unmeasured.

The 62,608-byte proposed combined A1/A2/I1/control layout has only 2,928 bytes of slack against 64 KiB. The estimated complete payload is 717,968 bytes before other stream overhead. The authoritative ceiling is .8 MiB prepared stereo. Measure exact layout and allocator counts across edge rates and stereo lanes, including finite/range guards, oscillator rephasing, logical validity resets, active subnormal processing and inactive-state retirement. Prepared binary64 calculations must expose ordinary source rejection, deterministic grid ties, true wet faults and safe conversion at the f32 boundary. Explicit neutral bypass has zero declared host transport latency. For V18 require zero allocation, reallocation and frees after warmup and active near-denormal/timing behavior. V19 preserves sample-clock transitions, ≤1e−6 FS partition parity and exact reset repeatability. V20 preserves the seven-rate <3 dB nominal spread, one-hop-plus-two-sample control duration bound and documented fallback/extreme-rate guards. For V21 the M2 Pro stereo budgets include <133 μs mean and <667 μs p99.9 at 48 kHz/64, <66.7 μs mean at 192 kHz/64 and no missed deadlines, with a hard 5.21 μs at 192 kHz/1.
