## INFORMATIONAL · Historical taps, storage and the full prescribed retune

**Requirement and derivation.** At the start of tick n, a delay D reads physical time t=n−D. Its bracketing integers are i=n−ceil(D) and i+1 for fractional D. The distance from i is w=ceil(D)−D, so the direct interpolant is U[i]+w(U[i+1]−U[i]). Writing D=m+u with integer m and 0≤u<1 makes this u·U[n−m−1]+(1−u)·U[n−m]. At integer D only U[n−D] contributes. For FIR preload at n−s, both sample times move back s while the weights retain the original D fraction. The fixed-history limits as D approaches integer k from either side both give U[n−k]. The outputs of successive ticks can still differ because the history advances.

The last C writes at tick n occupy the slots for n−C through n−1. With wᵣ the next write slot, the slot for lag l is wᵣ−l if nonnegative and C+wᵣ−l otherwise. The cursor is always below C. Validation gives `1≤D≤C−s` even for an arbitrarily large unsigned offset s because the subtraction saturates. For integer D the single lag is ≤C. For fractional D, m<C−s, so the older lag m+s+1 is also ≤C. The wrapped slot is in `0..C` in either branch. In particular l=C maps to wᵣ and is readable until commit. The actual read path skips the zero-weight neighbor, and commit stores once, wraps after the last slot, and saturates the count. **Complete capacity bound.** Let P=fs/f. Its minimum is 32000/1320 and at any supported rate its maximum is fs/65. The minimum delays of the bridge, nut and pickup are approximately 2.078788, 21.163636 and 1.539394 samples. The oldest requested times use delays `.127P+1` for the bridge including the two-sample FIR preload, `.873P` for the nut and `.0635P` for the pickup. The largest integer lag for each is the ceiling of its effective delay. All three are ≤ceil(fs/65)+1 for every supported f and fs, leaving at least three additional slots with the prescribed C=ceil(fs/65)+4. At 192 kHz the respective lag maxima are 377, 2579 and 188, with C=2958. At 32 kHz C=497. At any rate transition in capacity this proof has far more slack than quotient rounding. The conservative allocation therefore also covers fractional host rates. Native arm64 metadata was 72 bytes per arm, giving 8,096–47,472 bytes for two arms including wave payload and excluding allocator bookkeeping. ## INFORMATIONAL · Epoch validity, transaction ownership and sample cost

**Requirement and location.** Before count saturates, a missing lag has time count−lag=n−lag, where n counts successful writes since launch. After saturation every legal lag is stored. The M4 bridge seed uses `(V/β)·S((count−lag)/P)`; the nut uses the negative amplitude and phase shifted by 1−β. The seed records launch P and V. Reads at a new frequency evaluate the original epoch with the new tap offsets. Prepare sets an invalid epoch. Invalidate clears the epoch, cursor and count in constant time, and a successful launch hides stale slots by resetting the count before any reads. Rejected taps, controls and outgoing values are checked before their prospective mutation. ## INFORMATIONAL · Absolute V03 timing gate rejects the previous false pass

**Requirement and derivation.** The two-tap delay has transfer `exp(−iωm)((1−u)+u exp(−iω))`. The signed bridge FIR is `−.98 exp(−iω)(.75+.25 cos ω)`, and the nut contributes the other reflection sign. The FIR contributes one sample of delay with no extra winding in the test band. M2 therefore sets D_B+D_N+1=P, with a small frequency-dependent fractional phase residual. In the corrected sparse oracle the first nonzero arrival k preserves the time origin. The residual taps span at most four samples with nonnegative gains after both reflections. Their phase span is <π from DC to the highest test ω, so the residual sum remains nonzero and its argument stays continuous in that sector. The absolute delay is k−arg(residual)/ω. The expected P appears only in the acceptance comparison. A phase comparison at f₀ alone has period P because ωP=2π. Indeed the old residual formula gives an error of only 4.87e−13 samples or less for a constant .98 at tick zero.
