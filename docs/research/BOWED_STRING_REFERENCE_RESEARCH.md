# Reference implementations and evidence for the design

The selected source model combines STK's folded two-arm string with a force–velocity contact solved from the returning waves. Faust independently uses the same contact topology and supplies a convenient linear-phase loss filter. DDSP supplies a control principle: estimate performance cues on frames and render continuous audio between frames. ElectroBow will implement these equations in Rust. The reference projects were inspected from source checkouts on 2026-11-23.

## STK — the folded waveguide

Revision `6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc`. In the scalar production instrument tick, let B and N denote the previous bridge-side and neck-side delay outputs, and let H denote the string loss filter. One sample proceeds in this order. [Bowed.h, tick, lines 104–125](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/include/Bowed.h#L104-L125)

1. Compute bow speed V as envelope times maximum speed. Form incoming velocity waves b=−H(B), a=−N and free string velocity a+b. Each arm represents a trip from bow to its termination and back.
2. Compute the incident velocity mismatch d=V−a−b. With contact active, j=d·clip((.75+|s(d+o)|)⁻⁴,.01,.98), otherwise j=0. The offset o=.001 and initial slope s=3. The scalar table constructor sets those output bounds. The vector overloads differ, and `Bowed::tick` calls the scalar overload. This empirical reflection coefficient maps **incident mismatch** directly to added wave velocity. Treating it as a physical force law evaluated on actual slip would solve a different model. [BowTable.h, constructor and scalar tick](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/include/BowTable.h#L24-L98)
3. Write b+j to the neck and a+j to the bridge. Optional sinusoidal vibrato changes the neck delay. The output is the bridge delay output through six cascaded biquads times .1248. The biquads are outside the feedback loop. An enveloped moving bow does work repeatedly, including after a note's onset. At small mismatch the table makes contact velocity approach V, at larger mismatch it lets the string slip. It has no stored rosin state or iterative contact solve. [Bowed.cpp, initialization and body coefficients](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/src/Bowed.cpp#L32-L76)
4. STK sets total nominal delay to fs/f−4 with a small positive fallback. The bridge fraction defaults to .127236. `DelayL` writes then interpolates two adjacent samples. Reading `lastOut` before ticking incurs state timing already included in STK's approximation. The bridge reflection is a one-pole low-pass with DC gain .95 at ordinary audio rates and pole .75−.2·22050/fs. The other reflection inverts without filtering. Pressure maps to s=5−4p, p=0 can lift the bow. `startBowing` maps the amplitude to Vmax=.03+.2·amplitude, and `stopBowing` releases the envelope. Bounds on the coefficient, passive returns and fractional interpolation temper growth. They do not guarantee the desired oscillation mode for arbitrary gestures. [Frequency and bow controls](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/src/Bowed.cpp#L91-L171), [DelayL](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/include/DelayL.h#L120-L179), [OnePole coefficients](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/src/OnePole.cpp#L32-L49)

Use the arm routing, velocity-wave signs and bounded fractional read. Compute timing for the Rust ring convention. STK's approximate delay offset, controller messages, instrument envelope, body cascade and exception handling belong to its implementation. The [STK license](https://github.com/thestk/stk/blob/6aacd357d76250bb7da2b1ddf675651828784bbc/LICENSE) permits modification and redistribution with copyright and permission notice retained for copies or substantial portions. The upstream file also contains a historical patent note. No STK dependency is required by this design.

## Faust — explicit traveling paths and terminations

Revision `cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b`, `physmodels.lib` declares 1.2.14. `chain` composes bidirectional blocks with explicit feedback delays. In `waveguideFd4` two signals propagate on distinct delays while a third bus passes through. `stringSegment` computes each one-way delay as half the library's length-to-samples conversion. The library's string-length coordinate uses its `l2s` convention. A length value here is not a measurement of guitar scale length and string tension. `waveguide` selects fourth-order time-varying Lagrange interpolation (five taps). Its polynomial coefficients can have negative weights. The convex two-tap bound used in ElectroBow therefore cannot be assumed for this library. [Conversions and chain](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/physmodels.lib#L195-L272), [guides and string segment](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/physmodels.lib#L640-L817), [Lagrange implementation](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/delays.lib#L340-L403)

`violinBow` sums both arriving waves, subtracts the sum from bow speed, computes j=d·min(1,(.75+|(5−4p)d|)⁻⁴) and duplicates j. `bowInteraction` adds these corrections to the through-going waves. The pressure is a normalized table control. The .001 bias and .01/.98 clamps of the STK scalar path are absent. This allows exact sticking at small mismatch and arbitrarily small table coefficients at large mismatch. The signs of the mismatch and inverted terminating reflections agree with the STK velocity interpretation. Swapping the order of the left- and right-going signals changes port labels, not the contact force sign. Faust's bow position measures the nut-side fraction in `ntbd=length·position`; STK's beta measures the bridge-side fraction. [Bow tables, interaction and segments](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/physmodels.lib#L2003-L2140)

For brightness h and absorption A, `bridgeFilter` uses ρ[(1−h)(x+x″)/4+(1+h)x′/2], ρ=.001^[1/(320·20·(1−A))]. Both violin terminations invert and attenuate. The nut uses (h,A)=(.6,.1), bridge (.2,.9). Normalized nonnegative FIR coefficients sum to ρ and contribute one sample of phase delay in the passband. Bridge transmittance is identity. `violinBody` uses a 500 Hz bandpass, Q=2, on the transmitted path. The complete `violinModel` subtracts its measured 12 samples of chain/termination/filter delay through the length conversion. These twelve samples and STK's four are graph-specific. For ElectroBow one known FIR phase sample and explicit ring reads make the tuning auditable. [FIR](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/physmodels.lib#L701-L708), [terminations, body and full model](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/physmodels.lib#L2166-L2272)

The header grants LGPL-2.1-or-later with a special exception for code generated from a larger Faust program importing the library. That wording concerns the Faust compilation route. Write the Rust filters from the mathematical definitions and keep provenance, without manually porting a licensed library body under an assumed exception. [Per-file license and version](https://github.com/grame-cncm/faustlibraries/blob/cd7555d111e7f35a981af96068dea8b44d33607b/physmodels.lib#L44-L85)

## A force law for the contact

McIntyre, Schumacher and Woodhouse describe a nonlinear excitation coupled to a linear returning-wave history, multiple regimes and pressure-dependent pitch flattening. Smith derives the bow junction in velocity waves. Woodhouse's worked simulation gives v=v_history+F/(2Z) and a measured-data fit for the sliding coefficient. Joining that curve to the static-friction interval introduces cases in which stick and slip are both admissible. Desvages explains choosing the previous branch and discarding the unstable intersection. ElectroBow specifies a piecewise-linear discretization and an exact, bounded segment solve, including a deterministic initial history. The result can be tested against a force residual and a passive-string oracle. It generalizes the resolved reflection table in both toolkits and introduces an explicit normal force. [McIntyre et al., JASA 74 (1983), 1325–1345](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/mem2/papers/MUS-ACOUST/mci-schu-woodh-jasa83.pdf), [Smith, The Bow-String Scattering Junction](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Bow_String_Scattering_Junction.html), [Woodhouse, §9.2.1, eqs. 4–5](https://euphonics.org/9-2-1-time-domain-simulation-of-a-bowed-string/), [Desvages, 2018, §4, eqs. 4.76–4.77 and Fig. 4.9](https://www.acoustics.ed.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/Theses/Desvages_Charlotte__PhDThesis_UniversityOfEdinburgh_2018.pdf)

These equations assume a point bow and single polarization. In measured rosin friction, sliding force also depends on contact history. A fitted speed-only law is an intentional approximation. A sampled string can produce stable periodic, multiple-slip, raucous or weak motion even with bounded force. Those states must be distinguished in validation. The papers inform independent equations. Their text, figures, recorded data and code are not redistribution inputs. [Woodhouse, Schumacher and Garoff, JASA 108 (2000), 357–368](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.429529), [Raman/Helmholtz waveform construction](https://euphonics.org/9-1-2-ramans-argument-for-bowed-string-waveforms/)

A freeze would preserve a captured spectrum but would need a further mechanism for continuously tracked gestures. Additive resynthesis would require estimating or predicting harmonic envelopes and their changes under bow force. A feedback delay or fixed modal bank extends prior energy with its own resonances. The chosen two-arm oscillator directly ties harmonic period, relative velocity, pressure and losses together at constant bounded audio-rate cost. Its cost is the need for reliable monophonic acquisition and a calibrated playing range. A shaped direct path retains the player's pickup, attack timing and ambiguous/polyphonic material.
